I actually think this idea has far more potential than a typical college project—but only if you treat it as a research and engineering project, not just another web app.

Also, here's something interesting:

India is already moving toward digital water management.

That means you're not inventing a fake problem. You're building on a real national need.

The difference is:

Government projects focus on infrastructure and monitoring.
You can focus on AI-driven decision support, predictive analytics, and user-friendly tools.
Start by understanding existing initiatives

These should become your first references.

1. Jal Jeevan Mission (JJM)

This is India's flagship drinking water program.

Study:

Water quality
Rural water supply
Monitoring systems
Testing procedures
Water Quality Management Information System (WQMIS)

It will give you the real-world context for your project.

2. National Water Data Portal (NWDP)

This is a gold mine.

It contains datasets such as:

Water Quality
Sensor-based IoT pilots
GIS water supply
Water testing reports

You can use these datasets to simulate or evaluate your project instead of inventing fake data.

3. Smart Technologies in Jal Jeevan Mission

Read this research chapter:

Jal Jeevan Mission and the role of smart technologies in achieving water security in rural India

It discusses:

IoT
AI
Smart meters
Cloud dashboards
Water quality monitoring
Water conservation

This is probably the single most relevant research reference for your idea.

4. National Framework for Real-Time Water Quality Monitoring

This explains how India approaches:

Water quality
Real-time monitoring
Laboratory testing
Field testing

It helps you understand what exists today and where software could add value.

Here's why I'm excited about this idea for you

Most students build software.

Very few students build a digital infrastructure platform.

Imagine explaining this in an interview:

"I studied India's Jal Jeevan Mission, analyzed public water datasets, reviewed research on IoT-based water monitoring, and built an AI-powered Water Intelligence Platform that predicts consumption, detects leaks, and visualizes water quality using real-world architecture."

That's a much richer engineering story than "I built a water management website."

One suggestion

If you decide to pursue this, don't stop at the project. Treat it like a research-backed product.

We can design it the way large companies do:

Phase 1: Research (50–100 papers, government initiatives, datasets, existing systems)
Phase 2: Product Requirements Document (PRD)
Phase 3: System architecture
Phase 4: MVP
Phase 5: AI models
Phase 6: IoT integration
Phase 7: Cloud deployment
Phase 8: Publish a technical paper and build a production-quality portfolio.

That approach would give you both a standout final-year project and a strong demonstration of software engineering and product thinking.
