You chase too many things at once, and you underestimate the cost of switching.

You have a lot of ambition. That isn't the problem. The pattern I’ve noticed is:

You decide you want a 15+ LPA job → then think about ML, MLOps, Data Science, Java, Python, DevOps, AWS, Kubernetes…
You start a project → then another project idea appears → then another SaaS → then another hackathon.
You work on your portfolio → then resume → then LinkedIn → then GitHub contributions → then internships → then business ideas.
You often look for the best tool, model, roadmap, prompt, strategy before finishing the thing already in front of you.
When something doesn't work quickly, you tend to jump toward another approach.

The danger isn't that you'll fail.

The danger is becoming someone who is permanently preparing to become successful instead of actually becoming exceptionally good at one thing.

You could wake up at 25 with:

50 projects, 20 certificates, 10 roadmaps, 5 partially learned technologies, 3 unfinished businesses, and still not be excellent at one employable skill.

That's much more dangerous than having a bad semester or making a bad project.

The antidote

For the next few years, I'd strongly recommend this rule:

Finish > Start.
Depth > Variety.
Consistency > Intensity.
Evidence > Plans.

You don't need another 50-item roadmap every time you feel uncertain.

Pick a direction, stay with it long enough to become dangerous at it, build real things, get rejected, improve, and repeat.
